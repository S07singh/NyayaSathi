SMALL BUSINESS CONTRACT LEGAL REFERENCE
==========================================

Common Provisions and Business Rights under Indian Law
(Indian Contract Act, 1872; Sale of Goods Act, 1930; Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996)

1. SCOPE OF WORK
   - Deliverables must be clearly defined with measurable acceptance criteria.
   - Change order procedures should be documented.
   - Timeline and milestones must be explicitly stated.
   - Ambiguous scope descriptions create dispute risk.

2. PAYMENT TERMS
   - Payment milestones should be tied to deliverables.
   - Standard payment terms: 30-60 days from invoice date.
   - Late payment interest should be specified (typically 12-18% per annum).
   - Advance payment terms and conditions must be clear.

3. LIABILITY AND INDEMNIFICATION
   - Liability caps should be reasonable (typically contract value or 2x contract value).
   - Unlimited liability clauses are high risk and often negotiable.
   - Indemnification should be mutual, not one-sided.
   - Carve-outs for IP infringement and confidentiality breaches are standard.

4. INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY
   - IP ownership of deliverables must be explicitly assigned.
   - Pre-existing IP should be clearly excluded with license grants.
   - Background IP rights should be preserved for the service provider.
   - IP warranties (non-infringement) should be included.

5. TERMINATION
   - Both parties should have equal termination rights.
   - Termination for convenience should require reasonable notice (30-60 days).
   - Termination for cause should include cure period (15-30 days).
   - Post-termination obligations (data return, transition) should be specified.

6. CONFIDENTIALITY
   - Duration should be defined (typically 2-5 years post-termination).
   - Exceptions for publicly available information must be included.
   - Return or destruction of confidential materials should be required.

7. WARRANTIES
   - Warranty period should be at least 30-90 days post-delivery.
   - Warranty remedies (repair, replace, refund) must be specified.
   - Disclaimer of implied warranties should be carefully reviewed.

8. DISPUTE RESOLUTION
   - Arbitration is generally faster and more cost-effective than litigation.
   - Seat of arbitration should be mutually convenient.
   - Governing law and jurisdiction must be specified.
   - Mediation as a first step before arbitration is recommended.

9. FORCE MAJEURE
   - Events covered should be specifically listed (not just generic "Act of God").
   - Notice requirements during force majeure should be defined.
   - Both parties should have right to terminate if force majeure persists beyond a defined period.
